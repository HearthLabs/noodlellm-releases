What it does for you

It reads your own files

Point it at a folder of Word documents, PDFs, spreadsheets, slides or notes and ask about them. Before reading, it tells you how long it will take and which files it has to skip, and every answer names the file it came from.

It remembers you

Tell it once who you are and how you like answers written, and every conversation starts from there. It is a box you can read, edit and empty, and starting a message with "remember:" adds to it.

Long conversations do not fall apart

When a chat grows longer than the model can hold, it condenses the earlier messages into a summary instead of forgetting them, so names, figures and decisions from the start still count. You can read that summary at any time.

Set each chat up for a job

Give a conversation its own instructions, such as a tutor that asks one question at a time or a formal tone for client letters, and choose how steady or inventive its answers are. There are examples to start from.

Use it from your phone

Pair your phone once by scanning a code, then carry on your conversations from the sofa or the next room, with your files included. It only works on your home or office Wi-Fi and everything is encrypted between the two, so nothing passes through us. The phone app is coming soon.

In your language

The app speaks English, German, Spanish, Japanese and Chinese, and most models answer in whichever language you write in.

What it can and cannot read

It reads

  • Word documents (.docx)
  • PDFs with selectable text
  • PowerPoint decks (.pptx)
  • Excel spreadsheets (.xlsx)
  • Plain text, Markdown, CSV, JSON and RTF

It does not, yet

  • Scans and photographs of pagesThere is no text in the file to read, only a picture of it.
  • The pre-2007 .doc, .xls and .ppt formatsRe-saving one in Office as .docx fixes it.
  • Pages, Numbers and KeynoteExporting to the Word, Excel or PowerPoint equivalent works.
  • Images, audio and videoText only, for now.

Why run it yourself

The more of your work depends on AI, the more it matters who is in control of it.

  1. 01

    They set the terms. You live with them.

    Cloud AI companies decide what it costs, how much you can use, which countries can use it and which model you get, and they change all of it when it suits them. On your own computer nothing changes unless you change it: the model you have stays exactly as it is, and when a better one comes out you download it, with no new contract.

  2. 02

    Try again as often as you like.

    Good results come from rewording, retrying and refining. When every attempt is metered, you hold back. On your own computer the tenth try costs the same as the first: nothing extra.

  3. 03

    Your files never leave the room.

    Nothing you type or open is uploaded, so there is nothing to leak and nobody else's privacy policy to trust. Once a model is installed it does not need the internet at all.

  4. 04

    The cloud is smarter. That was never the whole question.

    Some jobs need the cleverest model there is. Most do not: drafting a letter, summarising a report, finding the clause in a contract. Your local AI tends to be weaker at hard reasoning and slower on a modest PC, so keep a cloud AI (e.g., ChatGPT) for the rare problem that needs the very best, and do the everyday, the private and the repetitive here.
